This planned project explores under which conditions relations between ethnic groups can be rebuilt after displaced communities return. 

It focuses on how **nationalist** and **transnational** understandings of ethnicity shape the risk of renewed **violence after return**, especially in Sub-Saharan Africa. 

To answer this question, I plan to leverage information on perpetrators' **justification** for ethnic cleansing from the [Geo-Located Ethnic Forced Displacement Dataset (GeoEFD)](https://www.janakissling.com/datasets), and comparative case analyses. 

This project aims to contribute theoretically and empirically novel findings that inform researchers and policymakers how narratives of **foreignness**, territorial **belonging**, and transnational **threat** can lock in the divisions created by displacement, and how such narratives can be overcome to improve prospects for return, reconciliation, and durable peace.
