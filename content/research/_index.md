My research examines **transnational identity** politics, **ethnic violence**, and post-conflict **peacebuilding**. 

I explore these topics combining **geo-spatial data** with quantitative **causal inference** and **mixed-methods** tools. 

Using these techniques, I help researchers and policymakers better understand: 

• How ethnic **identities** become **salient** across borders

• When political actors **mobilize** them for **violence**

• How they shape subsequent prospects for **peace**

{{< research-theme title="Transnational identity politics" theme="transnational" >}}

With polarization on the rise, ethnic violence is one of the most pressing challenges in today's world.

Rather than being contained within states, many affected communities span international borders, creating networks that connect governments, armed actors, and civilians across countries. 

In my dissertation, **The Causes and Consequences of Ethnic Cleansing: A Cross-Border Perspective**, funded by the [Swiss National Science Foundation](https://data.snf.ch/grants/grant/227100), I examine the transnational causes and consequences of ethnic violence, with a special focus on ethnic cleansing.

To investigate how transnational ethnic networks shape the risk of ethnic cleansing and its political consequences, I collect new geo-located data on ethnic cleansing across Africa, the Middle East, and Asia from 1990 to 2021 ([Geo-Located Ethnic Forced Displacement Dataset (GeoEFD)](https://www.janakissling.com/datasets)). 

I combine this dataset with maps of group settlements and geo-located surveys in a mixed-methods approach, using causal inference, spatial analytics, and process-tracing. 

**Three main findings:**

1) Ethnic groups with transnational links face a higher risk of ethnic cleansing, especially when they make claims for self-determination

2) Ethnic cleansing can strengthen ethnic identities across international borders and reduce attachment to national governments 

3) External armed support can intensify conflict in areas targeted by ethnic cleansing and trigger interstate disputes with kin states 

**Key take-aways:**

• **Research:** Transnational ethnic identities are not static but can be activated by nationalist claims and reinforced through experiences of violence

• **Policy:** Addressing transnational networks is relevant for mitigating the risk of ethnic violence and improving intergroup relations in its aftermath

{{< research-theme title="Spatial patterns of violence" theme="spatial" >}}

In ongoing work, I explore how ethnic violence unfolds locally and how it transforms affected communities in their social, economic, and political life. 

Understanding the local patterns of violence is crucial to mitigate its effects on post-conflict reconciliation and development.

{{< /research-theme >}}

{{< research-theme title="Peacebuilding" theme="peacebuilding" >}}


Why do some communities emerging from ethnic violence move toward renewed conflict while others achieve greater security and reconciliation? 

I explore this question focusing on how international peacebuilding can mitigate violence and rebuild intergroup relations after conflict.
